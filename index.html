CLAIM I

Safer trade route

The silk road is a safer trade route because traders can avoid the dangerous ocean strom and monsoons that are hard to predict.

Compared to…

Compared to the Indian Ocean trade route, the Silk Road does not depend on the seasonal monsoons and dangerous sea travel. Indian Ocean trade route merchants need to plan around the seasonal monsoons since the winds and storms can easily wreck their ships losing all resources. Even if there are no monsoons there can always be storms like heavy rains or floods that cause ships to break when trading on the water. The silk road merchants have a much safer and more reliable experience when trading since there are no monsoons and harsh ocean weather conditions.

Also compared to the Trans - Saharan trade route, the Silk road does not depend on harsh weather conditions like a dry desert and sandstorms. Merchants would have to cross massive stretches in the extreme heat of the Sahara, due to the heat, water was a rare occurrence making merchants very thirsty and travel was very difficult due to the sand terrain. On the other hand the Silk Road is much more simple for merchants to traverse since there are rest stops throughout the trade route, and the land is not as dry and does not lack water.

CLAIM II

More civilizations connected

The Silk Road connected more major civilizations and trading centers which gives merchants more opportunities to trade compared to other trade routes.

Compared to…

Compared to the Trans-Saharan, the Silk Road was far more connected to central civilizations. The Trans-Saharan trade routes connected kingdoms, but the kingdoms were very spread out and small in a relative manner. Most of these kingdoms did not have many goods due to their small size meaning they weren’t good to trade with on a major scale. The cities that were accessible to trade with on the Indian ocean were mostly port cities that couldn’t be accessed without ships and the major empires were more inland which wasn't easily accessible with a ship. The Silk Road had cities that were all accessible within the trade route that provided shelter and resources.

CLAIM III

Exclusive goods, higher profit

The Silk Road traded more items and exclusive items like silk that were more luxurious that were exclusive to the Silk road and therefore are higher profit while also providing trade for a large variety of items

Compared to…

The Trans-Saharan trade was solely based on products like gold, salt, and ivory therefore merchants don't have as much free will to sell their product. Because of the lack of variety in trading goods, if one item’s price dropped it would be catastrophic for merchants selling it because they have no other goods to diversify their supply to. On the other hand Indian ocean trade was also inefficient because it sold lots of bulk products but the product was very cheap leading to cheaper profit margin. Yet on the Silk Road all these problems were solved because it valued trading light valuable items which were great for profit margins this was achievable exclusively to the Silk Road because of China making their recipe for silk private. Not only that but the Silk Road also traded a mass variety of items due to its vast spread across the world attracting numerous markets for many items like spices in southern asia to glassware in Persia.
